# People Automations Partner: case study

Hi, and thanks for spending time on this case study for the People Automations Partner role at Apify! 👋

The case study mirrors the first months of the role: fix how company knowledge is organized, build automations that keep it current, and get team leaders to actually adopt the change.

This repo holds the context and data you'll need. The brief we sent you is the main reference for the assignment.

> **Estimated time:** We have no expectations on how long it should take you, but we'd like to know how long it took. Keep in mind our ways of working: a sharp 90/10 solution beats a polished one.
>
> **Deadline:** Submit five days after you receive the brief.
>
> **AI:** Use any tool as much as you like. We'd love to hear what you chose and why! In the debrief, we'll ask you to change a part of your solution live.
>
> **Format:** Submit your proposal in any way you think the work would be best presented. We'd like to see how your automation runs.

Feel free to use any other information you can find about us online.

## Materials

- **Example Notion pages:** a mockup of one of the main pages of our Notion wiki, linked in the brief. The content is made up, but the structure and type of information is exactly the same. Open the page, click **Duplicate** (top right) to copy it into your own free Notion workspace, and work on your copy.
- **This repo:**
  - `context/CONTEXT.md`: our Notion plan, its constraints and the tools you can use.
  - `data/notion_page_inventory.csv`: mockup data export from a part of our Notion workspace (154 pages). Columns are described in `data/README.md`.

## The tasks

### Part 1: Fix the Notion pages, then keep them fixed

**a) Diagnose.** Review the example pages and the inventory. What's wrong for a person, and what's wrong for an AI agent reading them? Prioritize: what would you fix first across all pages, and how would you decide that at scale?

**b) Fix.** Rework the example pages in your Notion copy so they look the way you'd want all the key pages to look. Explain how you would work towards the final content proposal.

**c) Build.** Design and **build a working automation** that will keep the pages up to date: assign owners, set review reminders and archive what is outdated.

### Part 2: Get it adopted

Your proposal from Part 1 is ready, and you'll pilot it with three teams. Three people reply on Slack:

> **Engineering lead:** "My team documents in GitHub. We're not going to maintain Notion pages on top of that, and nobody reads them anyway."
>
> **Sales lead:** "Love it in principle. But it's end of quarter and my team has zero time for this. Can People just do it for us?"
>
> **CEO:** "Good. Can we have this across the whole company within a month instead of piloting?"

Deliver:

1. **Your reply to each of them** as you'd post it in Slack, one message each.
2. **A one-page pilot plan:** which three teams and why, what you ask of each team leader, what you do yourself, and how you'll know after six weeks whether it worked.
3. **The company-wide announcement** of the pilot as a Slack post in #general.

## What happens next

A 60-minute debrief with us:

- **15 min:** you walk us through your solution.
- **15 min:** we ask for a change to your automation, and you make it live with whatever tools you'd normally use.
- **30 min:** role play. We'll play the team leaders from Part 2. Expect pushback.

## What we look at

- Did you find the problems that matter, and prioritize them well?
- Does what you built actually work, and would it survive real use?
- Did you fit the solution to our constraints and culture, rather than to an ideal world?
- Would a busy team leader say yes to your plan?
- Is your writing clear enough to act on without a follow-up meeting?
